# The একটুখানি Bangla writing guide

What the classics teach about writing short, warm, clear Bangla, and how to use it on a phone screen.

This guide sits on top of `research/bangla-voice-guide.md` (তুমি, word list, festivals) and `research/writing-brief.md` (the three tests, what to cut). Those rules still hold. This file explains *why* they work, using real Bangla prose, and adds the techniques they don't cover: rhythm, detail, humour, openings, endings.

Everything below comes from reading public-domain texts on bn.wikisource.org (list at the end). Quotes are short phrases, one per work at most. Most examples are new sentences written for this guide in modern Bangladeshi চলিত.

---

## 1. Core principles

1. **Say the thing, then stop.** The best lines in these books are plain statements. The strongest beat in পোস্টমাস্টার (রবীন্দ্রনাথ) is three words of dialogue: "আর আসব না।" No adjective, no comment.
2. **Feeling lives in objects, not adjectives.** Love in কাবুলিওয়ালা is a smudged handprint on a folded paper, "কাগজের উপর একটি ছোটো হাতের ছাপ" (রবীন্দ্রনাথ). Poverty in পথের পাঁচালী is a row of clay pots holding a little oil, a little salt, a little গুড়. The writer never says "love" or "poor".
3. **One sentence, one job.** Long sentences in these books carry description. Turns, decisions and feelings get short sentences.
4. **Trust the reader.** Not one of these writers explains a joke or spells out a moral the scene already shows. উপেন্দ্রকিশোর lets children see for themselves that the king ate a fried frog.
5. **Plain words beat learned words.** When রবীন্দ্রনাথ wants to mock bad schooling, he shows a translated sentence that sounds stiff: "ঘোড়া একটি মহৎ জন্তু" (শিক্ষার হেরফের). Bookish Bangla sounds like a translation, because it usually is one.
6. **Play is precise.** সুকুমার's nonsense works because every absurd step is counted, measured and priced. The joke sits in the exact number.
7. **Warmth is respect.** The tender characters (হেমাঙ্গিনী in মেজদিদি, রতন in পোস্টমাস্টার) show care with small acts: wiping a face with an আঁচল, fetching bath water at night. On our pages, warmth is a useful line that treats the reader as capable.

## 2. Rhythm

### What the classics do
- **Long, then short.** রবীন্দ্রনাথ builds a long sentence full of clauses, then drops a sentence of three or four words. After a long bargaining scene in কাবুলিওয়ালা comes a sentence of just three words saying something was bought. In ছুটি, a long fight ends in a three-word line: the game broke up. শরৎচন্দ্র does the same in মেজদিদি: a page of quarrelling, then one line saying the boy found shelter.
- **Short is the default for children.** টুনটুনির বই and হ য ব র ল run almost entirely on short sentences. হ য ব র ল opens with two words about the heat, then a lazy scene, then the surprise.
- **Dialogue as a script.** In পোস্টমাস্টার the farewell is set like a play: name, full stop, line. Name, line. It is the fastest, barest passage in the story, and the saddest.
- **Repetition as a beat.** In কাবুলিওয়ালা, Mini's sing-song call to the fruit seller comes back four times. Each time it means more. টুনটুনি repeats a whole greeting at every door, and children wait for it.
- **Sound words (ধ্বন্যাত্মক শব্দ).** Rain drips টপ্‌টপ্ into a clay dish, a lamp flickers মিট্‌মিট্, a cat laughs ফ্যাচ্‌ফ্যাচ্, mosquitoes go পীন্‌-পীন্‌. Bangla has a huge stock of these, and one of them can replace a whole adjective phrase.
- **Pauses.** The dash (—) marks a turn or a trailing-off. The "।" ends a thought firmly. Old texts often put "।" even after questions. We don't.

### Rules for us
- Keep most UI sentences to **4–12 words**. A lead may have one sentence up to ~18 words if the second is short.
- After any sentence over 15 words, write a short one.
- Use **"?"** for questions, **"।"** for statements, **"—"** for a turn, **at most one "!"** per screen, and **"…"** only for loading.
- Use a sound word when it is the most exact word: "টুপ করে পড়ল", "ঝুম বৃষ্টি", "টিকটিক করছে ঘড়ি". One per screen, and never two together.
- Repetition is allowed when it is a pattern (game refrains, a callback on the share card). It is not allowed as padding.

| Flat | With rhythm |
| --- | --- |
| তুমি তোমার নাম এবং জন্মতারিখ লিখে নিচের বোতামে চাপ দিলে তোমার বয়স বছর, মাস ও দিনে দেখানো হবে। | জন্মতারিখ দাও। বয়স দেখো বছর-মাস-দিনে। |
| তোমার উত্তরগুলো যাচাই করা হয়েছে এবং ফলাফল নিচে দেওয়া হলো। | ১০টায় ৮টা ঠিক। |

## 3. Feeling through detail

### What the classics do
- **One object carries the whole emotion.** The handprint in কাবুলিওয়ালা. The bath water রতন hauls from the river at night in পোস্টমাস্টার, because she doesn't know when he will leave. The kite, reel and fishing rod ফটিক gives his brother in ছুটি.
- **Show what people do, not what they feel.** In মেজদিদি, Kesto's tears drop onto his rice and he keeps eating. "বাঁ-হাতটা তুলিয়া চোখ মুছিতে পর্যন্ত সাহস করিল না" (শরৎচন্দ্র). That one action says everything about the house he has come to.
- **Childhood is a small, exact gaze.** In the first chapter of পথের পাঁচালী, a six-year-old watches each fistful of fried-rice powder travel from bowl to an old woman's mouth. Nobody says she is hungry. Later the old woman gets a cheap new চাদর and keeps telling people its price, "সাড়ে ন’ আনা দাম" (বিভূতিভূষণ). The repeated price shows her pride and how little she has.
- **Concrete nouns from daily life.** কাঁসার বাটি, মাটির ভাঁড়, ছেঁড়া কাঁথা, ঘুড়ি-লাটাই, পেয়ারাতলা, আঁচল. The reader recognises them and the feeling follows.
- **Weather and light do quiet work.** রবীন্দ্রনাথ sets partings in rain or autumn sun. He uses it as setting, not as a label for the feeling.
- **A warning:** রবীন্দ্রনাথ also writes long, ornate passages of তৎসম compounds about the sky and the "heart of the world". Those are of their time. Borrow the object, not the ornament.

### Rules for us
- When you want warmth, **name one thing** the reader has touched: টিফিন বাটি, হারিকেন, ঈদের নতুন জামার ভাঁজ, স্কুলের ঘণ্টা, নানাবাড়ির পুকুর.
- **Never stack feeling words** (আবেগ, ভালোবাসা, স্মৃতি, অনুভূতি, হৃদয়ের গভীর) on top of each other. One concrete noun beats three of them.
- In result lines, **a number is a detail.** "৩২ বছরে প্রায় ১১,৭০০ দিন" says more than "অনেক লম্বা সময়".
- Keep the detail true. The tool must actually give what the line names.

| Adjectives | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| আম্মুর জন্য তোমার গভীর ভালোবাসা প্রকাশ করো সুন্দর একটি কার্ডে। | আম্মুর নাম লেখো, এক লাইনে বলো কী মনে পড়ে। কার্ড তৈরি। |
| ছোটবেলার মধুর স্মৃতিতে ফিরে যাও! | ১৯৯৮ সাল বেছে নাও। দেখো, তখন এক কাপ চা কত ছিল। |

## 4. Humour

### How it lands in the classics
- **Absurd precision (সুকুমার রায়, হ য ব র ল).** A tailor's tape has worn off except the number 26, so every measurement comes out 26 inches. A crow says seven times two wasn't fourteen yet because time is money, and gives the gap in টাকা, আনা and পাই. The absurd idea is carried out with exact, serious accounting.
- **Changing what a word means.** A handkerchief turns into a cat, "ছিল রুমাল, হয়ে গেল একটা বেড়াল" (হ য ব র ল), and the cat calmly argues it could also be called চন্দ্রবিন্দু. A goat's name is ব্যাকরণ শিং because he can bleat (ব্যা) and has horns, and his B.A. means "ব্যা".
- **Word-building.** In খিচুড়ি a duck and a porcupine become a হাঁসজারু, and the poet shrugs "ব্যাকরণ মানি না". A singer invents মজারু because সজারু and কাঙ্গারু exist.
- **Deadpan.** Nobody in হ য ব র ল says something is funny. The narrator gets cross and argues back, and that is the joke.
- **Parody of officialese.** The crow's bill is a pile of court and land-office jargon. When asked what it means, the crow says this stuff simply has to be written, or the bill won't stand up in court. That is exactly how legalese privacy text sounds to our users.
- **Irony about "easy".** In খুড়োর কল the machine is "অতি সহজ এবং সোজা" (সুকুমার রায়), and you'll understand it after five hours of fiddling. In বুঝিয়ে বলা, an uncle promises to explain everything "in five minutes" and never gets to the point. Both are portraits of bad product copy.
- **Repetition with escalation (উপেন্দ্রকিশোর, টুনটুনির বই).** The tailorbird goes door to door. Every host gives the same welcome, "এস ভাই! বস ভাই!", and every host refuses, until a mosquito says yes and the whole chain collapses in one breath. The fox and crocodile split crops three times, and the crocodile always picks the wrong half.
- **The small beat the big.** Tuntuni teases a king. The fox traps the tiger. The humour targets power and greed, never the weak.
- **Gap between thought and speech (রবীন্দ্রনাথ).** In কাবুলিওয়ালা, asked a tricky family question by his daughter, the narrator gives one answer in his head and a different one aloud: "go play". Elsewhere he calls Rahmat's elephant joke "not especially subtle", which is a dry understatement.
- **Reversal (রোকেয়া, Sultana's Dream).** In Ladyland the women laugh that the visitor is "shy and timid like men". Men are kept indoors, and the argument for it is the same one used on women. The humour comes from calmly flipping the logic.

### What to borrow for result lines on cards
1. **Lead with the exact number or fact**, then add one dry observation. Don't add a third line.
2. **Take the game's logic seriously.** If a quiz says your "mental age" is 11, give a reason in the same deadpan register.
3. **Let a word wobble.** A tiny pun or coined word is fine if it reads aloud cleanly (তর্কবাজ → "তর্কবাজ-ইন-চিফ" is too much; "ভর্তা-বিশারদ" can work once).
4. **No laugh tracks.** No "হাহাহা", no 😂, no "মজার না?". If you have to say it's funny, it isn't.
5. **Punch up or sideways, never down.** Tease the situation (traffic, load-shedding, exam season), or the player gently, never a group.
6. **A callback beats a new joke.** Reuse a word from the question in the result.

| Loud | Dry |
| --- | --- |
| হাহাহা! তুমি তো পুরো পাগল! 😂😂 | মনের বয়স ১১। বৃষ্টি নামলে তুমি এখনো জানালার দিকে যাও। |
| অসাধারণ!!! তুমি জিনিয়াস! | ১০টায় ১০টা। এবার কঠিন সেটটা খেলে দেখো। |
| উফ! খুব খারাপ হয়েছে, আবার চেষ্টা করো! | ১০টায় ৩টা। লালবাগ কেল্লাটা প্রায় ধরে ফেলেছিলে। |

## 5. Dialogue and everyday speech

### What the classics do
- **Narration in সাধু, speech in চলিত.** রবীন্দ্রনাথ, শরৎচন্দ্র and বিভূতিভূষণ narrate in সাধু (করিয়া, হইল, তাহার), but their characters speak the way people talked. Even a hundred years ago, they knew real speech is চলিত. We go one step further: both narration and speech in modern চলিত.
- **Speech shows character.** In মেজদিদি, কাদম্বিনী sarcastically calls the orphan an honoured in-law, and that tells you who she is without a word of description. Rahmat's broken Bangla in কাবুলিওয়ালা is written with affection. The old aunt in পথের পাঁচালী speaks village Bangla full of clipped forms.
- **Plain speech tags.** হ য ব র ল uses "আমি বললাম", "বেড়াল বলল" over and over. The tag stays invisible so the words stand out.
- **Questions that sound real.** রতন's and ফটিক's questions are a handful of words each: a form of address, a verb, done. In today's Bangla that sounds like "আপু, ডাকছ?" or "কবে ফিরবে?". No connecting words.

### Translating their techniques into modern Bangladeshi চলিত
Take the technique, never the old forms. Modern equivalents:

| করিয়া / দেখিয়া | করে / দেখে |
| হইল / হইয়াছে | হলো / হয়েছে |
| তাহার / তাঁহাকে / ইহা | তার / তাঁকে / এটা |

### Register and address
- We always use **তুমি**. The classics show why the choice matters. A mother says তুই, a stranger says আপনি, and Rahmat's তুমি to a gentleman reads as charming. On our pages, তুমি is a friendly older sibling's voice. Don't slip into আপনি/করুন, and don't go down to তুই.
- **Dialect is for characters, not for the interface.** A game character may say "কিতা খবর" or "কেমন আছেন গো" if it is accurate and kind. Buttons, steps, FAQ and privacy stay in প্রমিত চলিত.
- **Use Bangladeshi everyday words:** পানি, গোসল, দাওয়াত, নাশতা, লবণ, মরিচ, আম্মু-আব্বু, খালা, ফুপু, চাচা. See the table in §6.

## 6. Word choice

### তৎসম vs তদ্ভব/দেশি
The classics mix both, but notice where each goes. Learned words come out for grand description or for irony (the narrator in কাবুলিওয়ালা calls his small daughter by a pompous Sanskrit word as a joke). Plain words carry speech, action and feeling. শিক্ষার হেরফের is entirely about the damage stiff, borrowed language does to children's minds.

**Rule:** use the word a person would say to a friend, or type into Google. Choose the learned word only when:
- it is the **official or established term** people will search for or see on a form (জন্ম নিবন্ধন, আবেদন, শতাংশ, ভগ্নাংশ);
- it is **more exact** and has no plain twin (আনুমানিক, সম্ভাব্য, নিরাপদ);
- it **protects** someone (a safety line must be unmistakable).

## 7. Openings and endings

### How these pieces open: one stroke
- **কাবুলিওয়ালা** starts with one character trait: a five-year-old girl who cannot stay quiet for a moment.
- **পোস্টমাস্টার** starts with a job and a place: a first posting, a tiny village.
- **ছুটি** starts with an idea popping into a boy's head.
- **হ য ব র ল** starts with two words about the heat.
- **টুনটুনি আর বিড়ালের কথা** starts by saying where the nest is: an eggplant plant behind a householder's house.
- **পথের পাঁচালী** starts with the house at the northern edge of a village.
- **মেজদিদি** starts by folding a mother's whole hard life and death into one sentence, then the orphan has nowhere to go.
- **Sultana's Dream** starts with an evening, an easy chair, a lazy thought.

None of them start with a preamble, a greeting, or an announcement of what's coming.

**Rule for page leads:** start with what the person gives and what they get. No "এখানে তুমি…", no "এই টুলটি দিয়ে…", no "চলো দেখি…".

### How they end: no lecture
- **কাবুলিওয়ালা** ends with a practical trade-off: to pay for Rahmat's trip home, the narrator cuts the electric lights and the band from the wedding. The feeling sits in the budget.
- **ছুটি** ends with the boy's last line, which echoes the title: "মামা, আমার ছুটি হয়েছে কি" (রবীন্দ্রনাথ) comes first, then the final words to his mother. There is no narrator comment.
- **টুনটুনি আর রাজার কথা** ends with an image: the king's men arrive and find only an empty nest.
- **বাঘের পালকি চড়া** ends in one flat sentence. That's it.
- **পোস্টমাস্টার** is the exception that proves the rule. After the boat leaves, রবীন্দ্রনাথ adds a paragraph of philosophy about the human heart. Modern readers remember রতন's plain lines, not the sermon. **We drop the sermon.**

**Rule for endings (result cards, FAQ answers, captions):** end on a fact, a number, an image, or an action. Never end on "মনে রাখবে…", "তাই…", "জীবনে…", or a proverb.

## 8. Children's simplicity without talking down

What টুনটুনির বই and হ য ব র ল do:
- **Short sentences, concrete nouns, clear cause and effect.** The crocodile takes the tops, and the potatoes are underground. A child can follow every step.
- **Simple verbs, mostly present or simple past.** "গেল", "বললে", "দেখল", "পালাল".
- **Explain only what is unknown, in one line.** Before the tiger walks into the trap, উপেন্দ্রকিশোর stops for two sentences to explain what a খোঁয়াড় is, then goes on. No more.
- **No baby talk.** Nobody says "সোনামণিরা". The child is trusted with irony (the king eats a frog, the tiger "enjoys the feast" inside a trap).
- **The child's logic is respected.** In হ য ব র ল the eight-year-old narrator argues seriously with a crow about arithmetic. The book never winks at grown-ups over his head.
- **Repetition gives children a handhold**, and payoffs reward them for remembering.

**Rule:** write so a ten-year-old in Rangpur can follow it and a grandmother in Sylhet doesn't feel patronised. Explain a term once, in a few words, where it first appears. Never "বাচ্চারা, চলো আমরা শিখি!".

## 11. Ten-point self-check

Read the line out loud, then ask:

1. **তুমি throughout?** No আপনি/করুন/দেখুন, no তুই.
2. **Modern চলিত only?** No করিয়া/হইল/তাহার, no গেলুম/করচে.
3. **Give → get, first sentence?** No "এখানে তুমি…", "এই টুলটি…", "চলো…".
4. **One job per sentence?** Anything over ~15 words is followed by a short sentence.
5. **A concrete detail instead of feeling words?** Swap আবেগ/স্মৃতি/ভালোবাসা for one object or number.
6. **Plain word where a plain word exists?** Check against the table in §6. Bangladeshi word choices (পানি, গোসল, দাওয়াত, নাশতা, ভাইয়া)?
7. **No exaggeration?** No অসাধারণ/দারুণ/সেরা, no "সহজেই", at most one "!", no emoji pile, no claim the tool can't back.
8. **Joke lands dry?** Fact first, one observation, no laugh track, no punching down.
9. **Ends on a fact, an image or an action?** No moral, proverb or "মনে রাখবে".
10. **True and complete?** Numbers, sources, estimates and privacy promises are exact. Simpler words, same meaning.

## Note on modern (copyrighted) writers

These books were not opened or quoted. A one-line note on each, from general knowledge:
- **হুমায়ূন আহমেদ:** very short sentences, everyday Dhaka speech, lots of white space, feeling from one small odd detail, and a light, slightly absurd humour.
- **সৈয়দ মুজতবা আলী:** dry, learned wit that wears its learning lightly. The joke is in the observation and the aside.
- **মুহম্মদ জাফর ইকবাল:** explains science and growing up to young readers in plain, respectful language, without talking down.

Their style matches what the public-domain classics teach.
